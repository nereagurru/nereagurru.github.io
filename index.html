Kaixo! I am Nerea Gurrutxaga.

I am a PhD researcher at the Max Planck Institute for Solar System Research in Göttingen, Germany. I study how planets form using computer simulations. Currently I am trying to figure out what meteorites tell us about the formation of our solar system. Learn about my research

Dust evolution in protoplanetary disks

Looking at how tiny dust grows into pebbles is the first stage of planet formation. I am co-developer of the Monte Carlo code mcdust, and I develop a method to strudy how we can track properties during dust growth.

Solar System formation

Once pebbles form, the next step is to form planetesimals, parent bodies of current asteroids, meteorites and comets, as well as the building block of planets! I use data from meteorites to test our planetesimal formation models.

Wide-orbit planet formation

Once we form the building blocks of planets, planetesimals grow even larger to form Eart-size planets or giant planets like Jupiter. However, the further we go from the star, the harder it is this growth. I study at what conditions the accretion of pebbles work in different protoplanetary disks to explain substructures and wide-orbit planets observed in other planetary systems.
